## Things the next stage should know

- Architecture risk 2 came true as predicted: Battle 3 has its own draw function (`b3Draw` in `engine/b3-play.js`). Do not route Battle 3 through `drawBattle`'s party loop.
- The screen follows a VIEW (`B3.view`), not the fight state. A beat that changes something the player sees must update the view in `b3Show`. Stage 1 had one bug of exactly this kind (a detonated Prime stayed over the monster's head). `b3ViewSync()` runs after every move as a safety net.
- Beats carry `g`: 'pre', a hit number or 'post'. A new op must stamp `g:c.g` on its beats or they show at the wrong moment.
- A status applied during the carrier's own turn does not count that turn (`skip`). The turn's wait is set before statuses count down.
- The simple player (`b3Auto` in `engine/b3-core.js`) scores moves in one small function. It never uses the random numbers, so it does not shift the fight's dice.
- Random numbers: only `b3Rand(F)`. A stray `Math.random()` in a rules file breaks the simulator-against-screen check.
- The Lab list: NEW BATTLE is first on every page, so the old fights' list positions moved by one. `tools/solo_test.py` picks by name now. Any other test that picks a Lab row by position must do the same.
- Party status chips sit on their own line under the name (`.b3-chips`); a monster's top 3 statuses are drawn over its head.
- No spoken lines exist for the six moves (`line:null` in the kit rows). Canon wants one per attack and Tom records them: owed, with the kit stages (architecture risk 8).
- Difficulty: the Lab still asks Rose, Grace or Director mode first; Battle 3 takes the mode's finished-number multipliers (`foeHp`, `taken`, `foeSpeed`) through `cfg.mods`.
