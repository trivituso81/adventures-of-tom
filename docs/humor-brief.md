## Brief

What Tom wants: irreverent, cheeky, goofy humor with small puns. Not sitcom (no setup, punchline, wait for the laugh track). Not children's show (no "silly billy", no explaining the joke, no lessons said out loud).
- Fast. Short lines. People cut in, answer a different question, or don't answer. Most lines are one or two short sentences.
- Two layers. Every exchange has a joke layer (silly comparisons, running bits, people doing the thing they always do) and a content layer (a fact, a clue, a worry). Keep both. The best place for a gag is right on top of a sincere line.
- Jokes come from character: their job, habits, history, what they would actually notice. If any character could say the line, rewrite it.
- A silly comparison is good when it's a real thing ("a giant cough drop"). Abstract labels are not ("the grey", "the warmth", "the stillness").
- Kids talk like kids: sharp, literal, blunt, things from their own life. A real kid only says what a real kid would know. Never poetic.
- Feelings come out sideways, as a detail. Nobody says the moral or names the feeling.
- One pun, then move on. Nobody laughs at their own joke.
- Still a family game: dry, sarcastic, silly. No profanity, nothing sexual, no cruelty, nobody humiliated.
