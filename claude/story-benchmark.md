# Story benchmark (Tom's marks, compiled 2026-11-17 from the Voice Studio export and his review notes). Every new line is tested against this. Add each new mark here.

## Failure patterns (apply to every speaker)
| Pattern | Rule it teaches |
|---|---|
| A line that answers nothing, or a question left unanswered | Each line answers the line before it |
| A pun that needs decoding, or a pun whose parts do not connect to the moment | A pun must make sense on first read |
| A mild wry button ("It's not easier in California", "I like almost everything") | Not a joke. Cut it and end on the last good line |
| Explaining a move or stretching a bit past its first laugh | Stop at the first laugh |
| Detail that does not connect to the point ("two in the afternoon") | Cut it |
| Monsters stating morals or long speeches | Monsters speak in short concrete sentences, no moral |
| Theme statements ("I have two homes. Okay. Good.", "That's what planes are for.") | Nobody states a theme or moral |
| The joke goes to the abstract thing (mud) instead of the lovable concrete thing (the muffins) | Joke goes to the concrete, lovable thing |
| TNB asking "what did he say?" about a funny image | TNB plays off the funny image instead |
| Tom mean, curt, condescending, or putting down TNB's puns | Tom is never the judge, never mean, enjoys the puns |
| TNB sweet-cute or effusive, exclamation marks | TNB is dry, mellow, low-key |
| Lines copied from a config example | Never. Build from the mechanism |
| Reference the reader would have to ask about | Cut it |
| Repeating a punchline or a name a second time | Say it once |

## Show-level
Short, physical, said in the moment, one clear image. Kids demanding things. Tom laughing at himself. Smirks and cheek, not children's-show sweetness and not mean jabs. Chapter 1 loved: "BOO!", "GAH! Rose! I almost fainted into Sal's hedge.", "Up, Tata! Up up up!", "Why are you three on the floor?" / "That wasn't Rose?".

## Exemplars (what makes a good joke here, mechanism only)
- Tom prepared (Knope): announces a plan or list for a small thing, with one absurdly specific detail.
- Tom bright side: names the bad thing plainly, then one concrete good thing in it.
- Tom polite fury: courteous words, one stressed word, aimed at a monster or object.
- TNB understatement: after a beat, answers something huge with one mild remark.
- TNB yes-and: believes Tom instantly and builds one small practical next step.
- TNB's own small thing: in a crisis he fixes on a small personal thing.
