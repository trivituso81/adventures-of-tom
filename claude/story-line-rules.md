# Line rules from Tom's notes on chapters 1 and 2 (updated 2026-11-20 18:30). Read before writing or fixing any dialogue.

The evidence behind every rule is claude/tom-line-edits.md (each of Tom's edits, before and after). Read both.

## The rules
1. A fact the story needs arrives inside the back and forth, never as its own announcement.
2. A character never names his own trait ("it's only polite", "Politely, and from the far end of a stick"). Tom: heavy-handed, not funny.
3. A trait shows in what the character chooses to care about in that moment, aimed at someone or something else. It does not show in a word about himself, and it does not show in a prop.
   - Wrong: "Poke it. Yes. With a very long stick." Generic. Anyone could say it. It is about Tom keeping himself safe, which is not his character.
   - Right (Tom's own example): "Poke it. Yes. But maybe just gently." He takes TNB's idea seriously and then worries about the cloud. That is his manners and his Leslie Knope earnestness, shown and not named.
4. When Tom flags a line, do not edit the flagged words. Throw the line away and write it again from what the character wants in that moment. Removing the bad word leaves a line with nothing in it (this is how "a very long stick" happened).
5. "To the moon and back" is a farewell, never a greeting (Tom, 2026-11-17, on chapter 1). Deedee says it when someone is leaving. She does not greet Tom with it, and Tom never says it back. The girls might say it back to her on the way out. A catchphrase belongs to the moment the real person uses it in, so check the moment before using any family phrase.

### Added 2026-11-20 from Tom's day of chapter 1 cuts (about 20 edits, 17 of them cuts)
6. **A first-time listener gets it with no help.** If the joke's link lives in the writer's head ("the size of a plum / I carried a suitcase through two airports", "a marble and it's winning", "lost at what?"), it is cut, not reworded. Check the facts behind the line too (the starter is already in Jefferson Park).
7. **No line restates what the player just saw or heard.** No reaction filler ("Your face is RED"), no recap ("I couldn't even make it wobble", "She did. I was right here"), no explaining a rule nobody asked about ("The girls stay home. Amy's rule").
8. **A scene ends the moment its job is done, and endings are the worst offender.** After the win or the decision: no callback pile-up, no showman bit, no second goodbye, no lap of honor. A transition is one plain line, usually Tom's, saying where they go next ("Alright, time for me to head back to LA. Let's head to Sal's!"). A functional line carries no tag joke ("Los Angeles? You got it." and stop).
9. **Warmth is plain.** A greeting is a greeting with energy ("Hi, Mom!"), not a wordplay echo ("I missed yours too"). A warm nickname ("kid") beats a ribbing one ("Mr. Fancy Pants").
10. **No setup lines for later chapters.** A line must earn its place in this scene. If its only job is to plant something for a later chapter (fingerprints, "Why pencil?"), it goes. If a later chapter needs it, that chapter sets it up in its own scene.
11. **Kids are real kids.** Grace and Rose say what a 9 and a 6 year old say: scared, bossy, gross, excited, simple words. Poop is funny. They do not signpost the mystery or speak in themes ("Because I don't know what any of it means yet"). Monsters and bosses speak in concrete words, not abstractions.
12. **Use the family's real word for the real thing** (muffin wrappers, not papers).
13. **Voice tags carry the voice.** Sal always has a heavy Long Island accent tag. Never capitalize a name for emphasis (TOM reads as "tomb"); use a tag. Every Tom line gets a tone tag; TNB stays mellow.

## The red pen pass (added 2026-11-20). Run it on every chapter before Tom sees or plays it.
Tom's day on chapter 1 was almost all cutting. So before any chapter reaches him, a separate reviewer (a fresh agent that did not write the lines) reads claude/tom-line-edits.md and then every line of the chapter, and marks each one KEEP, CUT or REWRITE with the rule number. Then:
- Cut what it marks CUT. Do not replace a cut line unless the scene stops making sense without it.
- Rewrite what it marks REWRITE by rule 4 (from what the character wants, not by editing words).
- Fix every line that refers to a line cut elsewhere (an orphan), in this chapter or a later one.
- Expect a cut of about one line in five. If the pass cuts almost nothing, it was too gentle: run it again with the table open.
- Record the counts (lines before, cut, rewritten) in the chapter's CHANGES entry, so the next play shows whether Tom's edits went down.

## Three tests for every joke line, before Tom sees it
| Test | Fail means |
|---|---|
| Could any other character say this line unchanged? | Generic. Rewrite. |
| Who or what is the speaker caring about in this line? | If the answer is "nothing" or "himself, by name", rewrite. |
| Does the line contain a word that describes the speaker's own settings (Tom: polite, proper, plan, organized, exact; TNB: chill, calm, mellow)? | Trait named. Rewrite. |
| (2026-11-20) Would Tom cut it? Compare against claude/tom-line-edits.md | Cut. |

## Why these failures happened (so the next session does not repeat them)
- Settings were treated as things to say, and lines were chosen because they were easy to cite as proof that a setting was used. The moon and back greeting in chapter 1 was the same mistake: the phrase was on Deedee's list, so it was used, in the wrong moment.
- Fixes were made by the smallest possible edit instead of a rewrite.
- (2026-11-20) The method said "write it short" (chapter 1 was 58 lines), but the locked chapter 1 came out at 95 lines and the checks only tested lines one by one for quality, never "does this line need to exist". Tom did the cutting himself. The red pen pass above is the missing step. Claude also defended setup lines against Gemini's cold read ("fingerprints sets up chapter 2"); Tom cut them. A reviewer's "this line does nothing" beats Claude's plan for a later payoff.
