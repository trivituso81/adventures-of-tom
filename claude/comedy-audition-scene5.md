# Scene 5 comedy audition: three takes (2026-11-13)

STATUS: waiting for Tom's pick. Nothing is built. Follows claude/next-addendum-dialogue2.md and dialogue3.md.

## How to judge (Tom)

The same scene written three times from a blank page: Marlowe takes the color and sound off the block, the family finds Mr. Washington in amber, they go check the neighbors. The three differ in who drives the scene and what the running bit is, not in wording. No line from the old drafts is reused.

Point at one take, or at single lines in any of them: which made you smirk, which made you roll your eyes. That is all that is needed. The other eight scenes get written from your pick.

Lines in [brackets] are staging, not spoken.

| Take | Who drives it | What kind of funny |
|---|---|---|
| A | Amy against Tom | Siblings bickering. A New Yorker who cannot tell LA-weird from actually wrong, and a brother who will defend his block through anything. |
| B | Marlowe | Deadpan paperwork. Marlowe treats the end of color like street cleaning, and the family argues the rules with him and loses. |
| C | Grace and Rose | The kids run tests while every adult has a confident wrong theory. Fast, short lines. |

## TAKE A: "Is that normal here?"

[Outside the loft. Amy stops on the sidewalk.]

**Amy:** There's a box of lemons on the curb. It says FREE.
**Tom:** That's normal.
**Amy:** What's wrong with them?
**Tom:** Nothing. People's trees make too many.
**Amy:** Nobody gives away good fruit, Tom.
**Grace:** Mom. That man's walking a cat. On a leash.
**Amy:** Tom?
**Tom:** Normal.
**TNB:** That's actually a really good cat.

[Down the block, a man in a grey suit writes on a clipboard. The house behind him goes grey.]

**Rose:** That house just turned off.
**Amy:** Is that normal?
**Tom:** They're probably painting.
**Amy:** The tree, Tom. They painted the tree?
**Tom:** It's a very committed block.

[The grey rolls up the street. A gardener's leaf blower is still going. No sound comes out of it.]

**Amy:** I can't hear the leaf blower. Normal?
**Tom:** No. But I've been asking for that since I moved in.
**TNB:** Babe. You wrote four emails.
**Tom:** Five. I didn't think anybody read them.

[The grey passes under their feet. The lemons go grey. So does the cat.]

**Rose:** The lemons turned off.
**Amy:** See, THAT'S what was wrong with them.
**TNB:** Still a good cat.

[The man in the grey suit reaches them. He does not look up from the clipboard.]

**Marlowe:** Mr. Rivituso. Five emails about noise. I read every one. Very nicely formatted. You're welcome.
**Tom:** I'd like to take them back.
**Marlowe:** Everybody does. Once it's quiet.
**Amy:** And you are?
**Marlowe:** Marlowe. I work for Mr. Vance. You'd be the family. He said you'd be standing exactly where I need to work.
**Amy:** Tom. Is HE normal?
**Tom:** No.
**Amy:** Thank you. One "no." That's all I wanted.
**Marlowe:** Check on your neighbors if you like. You won't be interrupting.

[He walks on. Across the street, one porch is still gold.]

**Rose:** That porch didn't turn off.

[Mr. Washington in his porch chair, inside a block of amber, pointing across the street.]

**Amy:** I'm not asking.
**Tom:** Not normal.
**Amy:** I said I'm not asking.
**TNB:** It's warm. Like the kiln the morning after. And it's hard all the way through.
**Rose:** Is he done? My bowl has to stay in till tomorrow.
**Tom:** That's Mr. Washington. He's pointing at my bins.
**Amy:** Your what?
**Tom:** My trash bins. They're still at the curb. He tells me every week. Six years.
**Amy:** Six years. About bins.
**Tom:** I figured he really cared about bins.
**Amy:** Tom. Nobody likes bins that much.
**Tom:** ...I should've brought them in.
**Grace:** There's the same light in the window next door. And the one after that.
**Amy:** Then we go door to door. Girls, with me. And nobody takes a lemon.

## TAKE B: "It was posted."

[Outside Tom's house. A new sign is bolted to the pole, under three parking signs. Marlowe stands below it with a clipboard.]

**Grace:** "No parking Thursday 8 to 10. Two hour parking 8 to 6. No color, no sound, noon to forever."
**Tom:** I read every sign on that pole. That one's new.
**Marlowe:** Posted seventy-two hours ago. You were at an airport.
**Tom:** ...That's fair.
**TNB:** Babe. Don't say "that's fair" to him.
**Amy:** Hi. Amy. Who's in charge of you?
**Marlowe:** Mr. Vance.
**Amy:** I'd like to speak to him.
**Marlowe:** So would I. There's a line. You're behind a mayor.

[He turns a page.]

**Marlowe:** Right. One block. Sixty-one houses. Somebody painted one purple, on purpose. Four dogs, a leaf blower, and a man who whistles. All of it goes.

[He ticks a box. The street goes grey, house by house. The sound goes with it.]

**Rose:** You turned off the bird.
**Marlowe:** The bird's fine. It just doesn't get to announce it.
**Grace:** You need a permit. Tata needed a permit just for a fence.
**Marlowe:** There's a form. Objections. Press hard, it's three pages stuck together.
**Grace:** "I object because it's not yours."
**Marlowe:** Mm. I get that one a lot. Usually spelled worse.
**Rose:** I drew a horse.
**Marlowe:** ...I'll file the horse.
**Tom:** What happens to the three pages?
**Marlowe:** White goes to Mr. Vance. Yellow's mine. Pink is yours.
**Tom:** The pink one's grey.
**Marlowe:** Yes. Sorry. That's sort of the whole job.
**TNB:** There was a guy with a leaf blower right there a minute ago.
**Marlowe:** He's fine. I don't do people. I do paint and noise. What happens after isn't my department.
**Amy:** What happens after?
**Marlowe:** Porch. Across the street.

[Mr. Washington on his porch, inside a block of amber, the only gold thing on the block. A glass of iced tea is halfway to his mouth.]

**Tom:** That's Mr. Washington.
**TNB:** It's warm. Like a car hood. And I can't dent it.
**Tom:** Is there a form for him?
**Marlowe:** No. I take the color, and the nice part of the afternoon's got nowhere to go. So it sets. Around whoever was having it.
**Rose:** He got stuck in the nice part?
**Marlowe:** Yes.
**Amy:** You did this to him.
**Marlowe:** It's a side effect. It's on the sign.
**Grace:** "Residents may set." It does say that. It's really, really small.
**Tom:** That's the smallest writing I've ever seen. That's not a warning. That's a secret.
**Amy:** How do we un-set him?
**Marlowe:** Don't know. Nobody's ever asked me to put one back.

[He checks his watch.]

**Marlowe:** I've got the next block at two. It was a nice afternoon here. You'll want to check the other porches.
**Rose:** Can I have my horse back?
**Marlowe:** No. It's filed.

[He goes.]

**TNB:** Next door's got the same glow in the window.
**Amy:** Then we knock on every door. Tom, bring your pink page.
**Tom:** It's grey.
**Amy:** Bring it anyway. It's the only thing he signed.

## TAKE C: "Test one."

[They step out of the loft. The whole street is grey. No birds, no cars. At the far end of the block a man in a grey suit writes on a clipboard.]

**Tom:** Huh. Marine layer.
**Amy:** The what?
**Tom:** Fog off the ocean. It burns off by noon.
**Grace:** Tata, we already had lunch.
**Tom:** Sometimes one.
**TNB:** Could be the power's out.
**Grace:** The sun isn't electric.
**TNB:** ...No. Good point.
**Amy:** It's me. I've been up since four.
**Rose:** Mine are doing it too. And I had a nap.
**Grace:** Hi everybody, welcome back. Today, something is wrong with Tata's street.
**Amy:** Grace. Not now.
**Grace:** Test one. My shoes are still pink. That mailbox was blue this morning. So it's not our eyes. Sorry, Mom.

[A dog behind a fence is barking hard. No sound.]

**Grace:** Test two. Rose, do the loud one.
**Rose:** BOO!
**Grace:** I heard that. I don't hear that dog. And his mouth is going.
**Tom:** That dog has barked every day since I moved in.
**TNB:** You said you wanted it to stop.
**Tom:** I wanted it to stop a normal amount.
**Rose:** There's a man.
**Amy:** Girls, stay here. Tom, go ask him. Nicely. He's got a clipboard, he's from the city.

[Tom walks down to Marlowe.]

**Tom:** Excuse me. Is this a gas company thing?
**Marlowe:** No.
**Tom:** Is it going to be long?
**Marlowe:** Yes.

[Tom walks back.]

**Tom:** He's not from the gas company.
**Amy:** That's all you got?
**Tom:** He wasn't chatty.

[Grace walks down to Marlowe. Rose follows.]

**Grace:** Hi. Did you do this?
**Marlowe:** Yes.
**Grace:** How?
**Marlowe:** I tick a box. The color goes to Mr. Vance. So does the noise.
**Rose:** Why?
**Marlowe:** It's my job. Excuse me. I've got another street.

[He goes. The girls walk back.]

**Grace:** His name's Marlowe, he works for Mr. Vance, and he did it with a pen.
**Amy:** You were over there ten seconds.
**Tom:** He wasn't chatty with me.
**Rose:** One porch is still on.

[Mr. Washington in his porch chair, inside a block of amber.]

**Tom:** Mr. Washington.
**Grace:** Test three. Rose.
**Amy:** Rose does not touch the...
**Rose:** It's warm.
**Grace:** Knock on it.
**Rose:** It's hard. And he didn't say come in.
**Tom:** We should call somebody.
**Amy:** Who? Who's the somebody for this?
**Tom:** ...The gas company?
**Rose:** He's in a soap. Like my dinosaur soap.
**TNB:** Her what?
**Grace:** Soap with a toy in the middle. You wash your hands until the toy comes out.
**Rose:** So we wash him.
**Grace:** Mine took a month. It was a stegosaurus. It wasn't worth it.
**Amy:** Grace.
**Grace:** I'm just saying. A month.
**Tom:** He asked me over for iced tea the week I moved in. I said "soon."
**Amy:** When was that?
**Tom:** Six years ago.
**Grace:** Same light next door. And the house after. Three is a pattern.
**Amy:** Okay. We check every house. Grace, you ask the questions.
**Tom:** I can ask questions.
**Amy:** You asked if he was the gas company.
