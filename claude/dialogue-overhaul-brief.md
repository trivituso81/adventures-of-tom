## Diagnosis: why try 3 sounded like kids' TV

1. "Plain words" was read as "simple writing". Every line is one short flat sentence in the same rhythm.
2. Jokes are cute and safe. They are announced, and nobody is ever the butt of one. There is no wit, irony or bite.
3. No subtext. Characters say exactly what they see and feel, and the rule is recapped out loud twice.
4. The adults have no adult inner life. Tom, TNB and Amy exist to answer the girls. No sibling history, no couple shorthand, no fear they are hiding from the kids.
5. The kids are written as cute. Real nine and six year olds are sharp, literal, ruthless and funny by accident.
6. Marlowe is a harmless eccentric. He has no menace and no sadness.

## Target (a proposal, to be confirmed by the tone audition below)

Family-film-for-adults register: writing that a nine year old follows and an adult enjoys for different reasons.

| Want | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Two-level jokes | The kid gets one joke, the adult gets a second one in the same line. |
| Subtext | People talk around the thing. An adult making a joke because he is scared. A line that means something else to the one person who knows the history. |
| Specific people | Tom and Amy are siblings with thirty-plus years of material. Tom and TNB are a couple with shorthand. Amy is competitive and a Special Education teacher who manages a room by reflex. Boppy and Deedee have a long marriage. Use it. |
| Sharp kids | Grace is bright and bossy and often right, which is annoying. Rose says the true thing nobody wanted said. Neither is "adorable". |
| Villains with weight | Vance and Marlowe are funny and also actually unsettling, and each is sad underneath. |
| Edge without anything inappropriate | Dryness, sarcasm, dark-ish humor about the situation, adults being imperfect. No profanity, nothing sexual, no cruelty, nothing a parent would wince at. |
| Earned warmth | The moral is never stated. Kind moments land because the scene before them had teeth. |
| Rhythm | Vary line length. Interruptions, half sentences, someone not answering. Not every line is a setup or a punchline. |

Still in force from the real-talk brief: react before explaining; nobody states what they could not know; rules of the world are found by trying; riddles from villains get questioned; no aphorisms standing in for plain statements. From the project rules: contractions, no em dashes, canon in docs/CANON.md and claude/canon-addendum.md, the Tom and TNB relationship reads caring (teasing is buffered by care), the girls say "Tata" in speech.
