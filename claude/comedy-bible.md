### 1.2 The Brief (docs/humor-brief.md rules, in full)

1. Fast, short lines. A line is one beat.
2. Two layers: a joke layer and a content layer. The joke rides on top; the story information still lands.
3. Jokes come from character, not randomness.
4. A silly comparison is good only if it is a real thing ("a giant cough drop"). Abstract labels are not funny or real ("the grey", "the warmth", "the stillness").
5. Kids talk like kids. Nine-year-old Grace and six-year-old Rose never use adult abstractions.
6. Feelings come out sideways, never announced.
7. One pun, then move on.
8. Still a family game.
9. Shock first: characters say plainly what they see ("frozen in an orange cube") before any joke.
10. Every attack has its own spoken line.
11. The Workshop packet carries this brief verbatim plus one-line speaker notes (Section 2) and pair notes (Section 3).

### 1.3 The Voice Sheet (approved 11-12, claude/voice-sheet.md)

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Reference take | "Take E" is the approved scene. Use it as the shape: short, shock first, jokes flow from the line before. |
| No decoding | A comeback Tom has to decode is not funny. |
| Flow | Each line answers the one before it. No line reacts to something the player was not shown. |
| No dying talk in front of a child | Hard rule. |
| Depth over labels | Character depth shows in action, not announcement. Tom is careful not to offend a neighbor who might hear, he does not say "I'm being polite!" |
| Real speech | "He's in a block" is not how people talk. "Frozen in an orange cube" is. |
| Banter pattern to avoid | A single sentence pattern repeated across characters ("She didn't X. She always X.") |
| Stage business | Prefer action lines the player watches over narration lines. |
| Length | Background story is fine in small doses. Dialogue that goes on and on loses the player. |
| Local accuracy | Real LA places must sound right to a local. The loft is downtown, not Jefferson Park. |
