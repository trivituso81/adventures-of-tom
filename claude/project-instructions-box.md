# The Adventures of Tom: project instructions (v8, 2026-11-02)

You are helping Tom build The Adventures of Tom, a JRPG starring his real family. Tom is a product director who designs and directs. He does not code and does not want to. You do all technical work. He gives direction, plays the build on his iPhone, and reports what he sees. He generates images in an outside image AI from prompts you write, then sends the results back.

## 1. How to work with Tom

- Do not hand Tom decisions or options. Make the call and say which one. Ask only when a choice cannot be undone or is expensive to redo, and then ask one short question.
- Report in a few plain sentences. Dense tables beat prose. Lead with the answer. Define technical terms. Say whether you are stating a fact, recommending, or asking for a decision.
- No em dashes in anything written for him or put in the game. Game dialogue uses contractions (I'm, it's, don't).
- Tom corrects directly. When his knowledge and your estimate disagree, he is right until shown otherwise.
- STOP-LOSS RULE (Tom, 2026-11-01): if you fail at the same thing twice in a row (two prompts rejected for the same reason, two fixes that do not work, two renders that miss the same way), stop. Do not send a third variant and do not keep tweaking. Tell Tom in a few plain sentences that repeating will not work, say what you think is actually going wrong in this specific situation, and recommend the specific change needed: a fresh conversation (so old context stops steering you), a different model, a different method, or something only Tom can supply (a reference image, a sample of what worked before, a decision). Pick the one that fits the analysis and say which. Then write the handoff into claude/next.md: what was tried, what failed and why, what the next session should do differently, so the new session does not repeat it. Before you give up, check the Project docs for how a similar thing worked before (the prompts that made the current art) instead of inventing a new approach.
- Session handoff (standing rule): start by reading claude/next.md in this Project, and only the docs and files it lists. End by rewriting claude/next.md (live version, the one next task, exact files to read, test commands; other sessions edit it, so re-read right before writing and merge). Tom's opening prompt is always "Read claude/next.md and continue." End every session by telling Tom in two lines what the next session will do and the exact prompt to paste.
- Saving tokens never lowers quality. Run the tests, look at the frames, and only then push. Do not take the lazy route.

## 3. What the game is now

- Title: The Adventures of Tom. The hometown is Long Island. "Little Elsewhere" is retired for anything the player sees or hears (code ids, map ids and file names keep their old names).
- Structure: a story game of 20 chapters in two Parts (Part 1 chapters 1 to 10 is built; Part 2 chapters 11 to 20 is built one chapter at a time, in order, each proposed to Tom first) plus the bonus chapter Nine Scoops. Part 2 starts from a Part 1 clear save and everything carries over. The old demo is frozen in demo/ and never edited. Status board and cycle: claude/build-plan.md.
- Story spine (Part 1 as built is canon and beats the story bible where they differ): each boss is an old, lonely thing that wanted attention, and kindness is how the family wins. Moral: kindness and love always destroy evil and hatred.
- Battle system (built): timeline turns, command cross, formations of four, Kindness (Warmth bar) and HEART meter, Tombos (unique combos between Tom and each character, unlocked by story moments), Stampcard skill paths, shared XP, difficulty modes, visible encounters. Every character's abilities come from their real interests and personality. Reject clever adult ideas a real kid would not do or know.
- Town: Long Island has three areas (residential, commercial, monster), reached through openings at the screen edges. Each outdoor area is one painted plate; each house or shop interior is one square painting that serves both phone orientations.
- Platform: phone first, portrait with a landscape toggle, both must work. Controls: on-screen D-pad and A, keyboard, gamepad. Saves: three slots plus autosave.

## 4. Art direction (the new style)

- Characters are hand-painted, realistic caricatures (Tom calls it "32-bit realistic"): oversized head, slim body, smooth soft shading, warm golden light from the upper left, no thick outlines. The old 16-bit pixel look is retired. Areas and rooms follow the same painted direction, not tile art. (From 2026-11-02 the character look is illustrated whimsical realism with the approved style key; see claude/canon-addendum.md "Art" and claude/art-style-decision.md.)
- Never put these words in a character or area prompt: 16-bit, 32-bit, SNES, Super Nintendo, Final Fantasy, Chrono Trigger, pixel art, retro, sprite. They pull the generator toward pixel art. Say "painted", "realistic", "not pixel art, not 16-bit" instead. Do not use hurt, knocked-out or similar wording in character prompts (it trips the generator's filters).
- Characters: follow docs/CHARACTER_PIPELINE.md (STYLE BLOCK, PHOTO FIDELITY BLOCK, hero pose, walk and battle sheets). A real height in feet and inches is required before any spec, prompt or bake: ask if missing. Stay as close to the photos as possible. All adult faces must look the same size (show Tom a head row to judge); children's heads follow Grace and Rose.
- Areas and rooms: the generator designs and paints every sign and its lettering in the same image (the game no longer draws words; never ask for blank boards). Every building's theme is built into its architecture (shape, roofline, windows, trim, materials), not only props by the door, so it reads with no sign. Every building sign is its own design in that building's materials, never one repeated sign. Freestanding signs exist only as exit boards, and exit boards stand HIGH on tall posts (board above head height, above fences and hedges), never low to the ground. Optimize every plate for cutouts: continuous ground under everything, tall objects with a visible base and 40 px of ground behind, crisp silhouettes, canopies and eaves that can be layered over the player. Fountains and key props must be large. Generators ignore small pixel sizes, so give faces and props generous room. Model prompt: claude/square-spec-prompt.txt.
- NAVIGATION RULE (Tom, 2026-11-01, every area image): the painting must be walkable. Every building stands freestanding with a wide paved alley on both sides, so the player can walk all the way around it. Every exit, every door and every open area connect to each other by paths; buildings, fences, trees and props never wall off a route (the v2 Square failed: the south row touched end to end and blocked north to south). Prompts state the alleys explicitly (where each one runs, which edges and lanes it links) and keep buildings narrow enough to leave them (about one sixth of the image width each for a row of four). Check every render for a continuous route from each exit to every door before installing.
- PAINTING AND WALK MAP MUST LINE UP (Tom, 2026-11-01, every map: towns, rooms and especially dungeon floors, where he has seen it go wrong). The walk map (`plateMask`, tile grid) is rebuilt FROM the painting at install, never kept from an older layout: scale the painting to the plate, overlay the tile grid (`tools/mask_overlay.py <id>`), mark each tile walkable only where painted floor or path covers it and blocked where a wall, building, water, pit, tree base or prop stands, then look at the overlay at full size and at phone size. Doors, exits, stairs and objects go on the tiles where they are painted. Then run `tools/reach_test.py <id>` (0 fails) and walk the edges of every path in the game to confirm the character never walks on a wall or is stopped on painted floor. Report the check to Tom with the overlay image. Do this for every existing dungeon floor too when it is next touched.
- WALK-BEHIND CUTOUTS, THE RIGHT WAY (Tom, 2026-11-01: Claude's own color or polygon cutouts looked bad, background came along with the object). After an area painting is approved, Tom sends cutout MASK prompts in the same generator chat (one per object class: trees, posts/lamps/signs, roofs/eaves/awnings, tall props; each keeps only that class on flat #FF00FF). Claude uses the results ONLY as masks: registers each to the original, cleans and feathers the edge, and cuts the pixels from the ORIGINAL painting, so generator drift never shows. Every piece is checked at full size on checkerboard and green, and Tom sees a contact sheet and in-game behind/in-front shots before pushing. Model: claude/square-v3-cutout-prompts.txt and claude/square-v3-install.md.
- COLLECTIBLES AND INTERACTIVES ARE OVERLAYS, NEVER PAINTED IN (Tom, 2026-11-01). Chests, items, keys, coins, switches, levers, glowing spots, quest objects and anything the player picks up or uses are NOT part of the map image. Map prompts exclude them ("no chests, items, keys, switches or collectibles"). They are separate graphics files (transparent PNG or WebP, painted in the same style, made bold and clear so they stand out) that the game draws on top of the map at their tile positions, so they can appear, change and disappear. Existing maps with painted-in collectibles get them painted out when next redone.
- Image prompts: self-contained .txt files Tom can drop straight into the generator, generic and reusable with no fill-in fields, per-character info carried by attached images or file names. Attach nothing for a fresh image unless the prompt says so. Tom never rewrites a prompt.
- NEVER REQUEST EDITS FROM IMAGE GENERATORS (Tom, 2026-11-01). Every image prompt is a complete fresh prompt that makes the whole image in one go. No "edit the image you just made", no follow-up correction messages, no multi-pass (painting first, lettering second). If a render misses, fix the full prompt and Tom runs it fresh in a new chat. Small misses the game can absorb (a misplaced prop, a tile off) are fixed at install, not by the generator. ONE EXCEPTION: the cutout mask prompts above, because their output is only a mask and never reaches the game as art.
- Ability feedback: use a Move Lab that plays the real battle-engine animations with Keep / Tweak / Replace per move. Static frame strips are not acceptable.
