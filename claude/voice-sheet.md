## The register in one line

B's jokes and speed, layered on top of C's content. The scene is funny out loud (the giant cough drop, Rose licking it, hands in pockets) AND it carries real information, history and worry underneath. Neither layer is dropped to make room for the other.

## Rules for every line

| Rule | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fast | Short lines. People cut in, answer a different question, or do not answer. Most pages are one or two short sentences. |
| Two layers | Every scene has a joke layer (silly comparisons, running bits, Amy bossing adults, Rose doing the thing) and a content layer (a fact, a worry, a piece of history, a true observation). Write both. A scene that is only gags is too thin; a scene that is only content is too heavy. |
| Jokes interrupt the serious part | The best spot for a gag is right on top of a sincere line (Tom's regret, then "I licked it."). The sincere line still counts. |
| Gags can carry facts | A bit pays off with information when it can ("Warm, hard, not lemon."). |
| Silly comparisons are welcome when they are a real thing | "A giant cough drop" is good: it is what the thing looks like. Abstract labels are not ("the yellow", "the grey", "the warmth", "the stillness"). This goes double for the kids (Tom, 2026-11-12). |
| Kids talk like kids | Sharp, literal, blunt. They use things from their own life (a rock book, a class rule, a sister's habit). Never poetic. |
| Adults have a life | Sibling history, couple shorthand, a 51-year marriage. Adults joke when they are scared and the kids notice. |
| Feelings sideways | Nobody says the moral or names the feeling outright. Regret, fear and love show up as a detail ("I've never once come up these steps"). |
| Still a family game | Dry, sarcastic, silly, a little dark about the situation. No profanity, nothing sexual, no cruelty, nobody is humiliated. |
| House rules | Contractions. No em dashes. Pages under about 175 characters. The girls say "Tata" (on-screen "Ta-Ta"). React before explaining. Nobody states what they could not know. |

## The people

**Tom.** Dry and calm, and the calm gets flatter the more worried he is. Jokes about: rules, policies, video-game logic, his own habits (lawn, skincare, sourdough, flying). Often the straight man to Amy and the girls. Avoids: saying he is scared, big speeches, anything dismissive toward TNB. When he is sincere it is short and specific.
- "I own a house." (when Amy tells him to put his hands in his pockets)
- "Six years he's waved at me. I've never once come up these steps."

**TNB.** Easy and unhurried, the one who actually touches the thing and reports back. Notices materials, how things are made, how they could be fixed. Jokes about: puns (one, then he moves on), horror-movie rules, transit, tallying up the facts of a ridiculous situation. Avoids: panic, sarcasm aimed at Tom. Calls Tom "babe". He and Tom never say "I love you"; it shows in who checks on whom.
- "Warm, hard, not lemon. That's three facts."
- "In a horror movie, this is where we split up. So we're not doing that."

**Amy.** Quick, competitive, runs any group like a classroom without noticing she is doing it, adults included. Names the absurd thing first. Jokes about: Tom, with thirty-plus years of evidence. Needles him and backs him in the same breath. Avoids: admitting she is tired or out of her depth. Long Island accent. Never "older" or "younger" sibling.
- "Who is that, and why is he in a giant cough drop?"
- "...Of course you did. And what did we learn?"

**Grace (9).** Bright, bossy, corrects people, usually right, which is the annoying part. Brings facts from books, school and videos, and offers them at the worst moment. Takes other people's jokes literally and fixes them. Avoids: admitting she is scared; she gets more factual instead.
- "It's not a cough drop. That bug is forty million years old."
- "I'm just saying how long it lasts."

**Rose (6).** Does the thing before anyone can stop her, reports it flatly, and then says the plain true thing the adults were stepping around. Short sentences, six-year-old words. On purpose her jokes are "Boo!" and silliness; by accident she has the best line in the scene. Avoids: nothing.
- "I licked it."
- "He's not happy. He's stuck. Your face can do both."

**Deedee.** Warm, firm, practical, a nurse for forty years who has seen worse and says so. Feeds first, asks second. Jokes about: Boppy, and herself only after someone else starts it (glasses, gas). Avoids: fuss about herself. "My favorite son!", "girls" (never "babies"), "to the moon and back", ROBERT when she means it. Long Island accent.
- "I was a nurse for forty years. Sit down, you're the wrong color."
- "Robert. Fifty-one years. I know what your scared laugh sounds like."

**Boppy.** Sweet, a joker and a complainer, never gruff. Complains about his stomach and people walking too fast so he does not have to say what is really bothering him. Laughs when he is scared. Fish get bigger every time he tells it. Avoids: saying he is afraid of getting old.
- "You people walk like the building's on fire. Is it? No? Then slow down."
- "It was a twelve pounder. ...Nine. A big six."

**Justin.** Direct, serious, few words, not warm. Laughs out loud once, when it is earned. Avoids: small talk.
- "I've got twenty minutes. What do you need lifted?"

**Julian Vance.** Courteous, patient, never raises his voice, talks like a very kind airline announcement. Believes he is helping. Funny because he is so reasonable and unsettling for the same reason. Sad underneath: he stopped the clock because of something he lost. Avoids: threats, riddles. The family is allowed to ask him plain questions (and make fun of him) and he answers plainly.
- "Nobody here is late. Nobody here is sad. I'd have thought you'd thank me."
- "You keep leaving people you love at airports, Tom. I only want you to stop."

**Monochrome Marlowe.** A tired fixer with a clipboard. Clipped, bored, professionally unimpressed, as if color is a mess someone left for him. Funny because he reviews a neighborhood like a bad hotel. Unsettling because he means it. Sad underneath: he only moves the colors around and has stopped asking why.
- "Too many porches. Somebody's always on one."
- "I don't take anything. I tidy. It's different. It has to be different."

**Nara.** Barks and whines only. People talk to her like she understands, and she does.
