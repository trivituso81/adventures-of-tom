## Prompt text

IMAGE REQUEST: ONE SINGLE ANIMATION SHEET. Return exactly one image, nothing else.

WHAT THIS IS
Animation frames for "The Adventures of Tom", a family role-playing game in the style of a 16-bit Super Nintendo game (Final Fantasy VI, Chrono Trigger). Frames 1 to 8 show Rose, a 6-year-old girl. Frames 9 to 16 show her two stuffed lambs flying. The attached reference images show Rose, Lamby and Backup Lamby. Draw all three EXACTLY like their references: same faces, colors, proportions, art style and level of detail.
- Lamby: soft plush toy lamb with cream curly wool, pink inner ears, big button eyes and a tiny pink bow tie.
- Backup Lamby: slightly smaller second plush lamb with lavender-grey wool, mismatched button eyes and one floppy ear.

THE MOVE
"Lamby Boomerang": Rose throws both lambs at a monster. They spin through it, loop around and fly back to her, and she catches them and hugs them. Silly, proud and joyful.

THE SHEET (strict)
- A grid of 4 columns by 4 rows of square cells, 256 x 256 pixels each, so the full image is 1024 x 1024. If your tool offers a different size, keep exactly 4 columns and 4 rows.
- Exactly 16 frames, one per cell, filled left to right, top to bottom, in the order of the numbered list below.
- Frames 1 to 8 (Rose): same character at the same scale in every cell. Standing Rose is about 170 pixels tall. Never rescale her per frame. Each frame sits fully inside its cell with at least 10 percent empty margin. Whenever her feet are on the ground, the soles rest on the same horizontal line, about 28 pixels above the bottom of the cell. Rose faces LEFT in every frame, in a side or three-quarter view from slightly above, like a party member in a 16-bit battle scene facing an enemy off to the left.
- Frames 9 to 16 (lambs): each lamb is drawn alone, centered in its cell, filling about 60 percent of the cell, with at least 15 percent empty margin. Lambs are drawn at the same size in all eight of these cells.
- Nothing touches or crosses a cell edge or overlaps a neighbouring cell.
- Background: one flat solid color, pure magenta #FF00FF, filling the whole image. No magenta anywhere in the characters. No grid lines, borders, cell backgrounds, numbers, labels, captions or titles.
- No ground shadow, dust, speed lines, sparkles, stars, motion blur, impact bursts or any effect floating outside the characters. Show motion through pose alone. No monster and no other people. No other props.
- In frames 1 and 2 and 7 and 8 Rose holds the lambs, drawn small and in the right colors. In frames 3 to 6 her hands are empty.

ART STYLE
- 32-bit era high-detail pixel art (late SNES, Saturn, PlayStation quality): rich shading, 24 to 32 colors, clean readable silhouette, no anti-aliasing blur, no outline thicker than one pixel.
- A crisp one-pixel darker rim around the outer edge and a faint lighter highlight along the top edge.
- Soft key light from the upper left, shadow on the lower right, warm slightly desaturated painterly palette. All frames share one look and one lighting direction.
- Family friendly for children aged 6 and up.

THE 16 FRAMES (each must look clearly different from the others)
1. WIND-UP A: standing with knees slightly bent, Lamby in one hand and Backup Lamby in the other, both held up by her shoulders, a determined little frown, tongue poking out the corner of her mouth, eyes locked on the target.
2. WIND-UP B: body twisted back, both arms swung back and down behind her with a lamb in each hand, weight on her back leg, cheeks puffed, eyes squinting with effort.
3. THROW: the moment of release, front foot stepped forward, both arms thrust forward and up to the left with fingers open and hands EMPTY, mouth wide open in a throwing yell.
4. FOLLOW-THROUGH: arms still extended to the left, leaning far forward, one leg kicked up behind her, hands empty, eyes wide and following the lambs, mouth open in a delighted "wheee".
5. WATCHING: standing, one hand shading her eyes as she looks left and slightly up, the other hand on her hip, mouth open in an "ooooh", weight on one leg.
6. REACH: up on tiptoe, both arms stretched high and to the left with open palms up, hands EMPTY, eager wide eyes and an open smile, ready to catch.
7. CATCH: both lambs clutched against her chest, one in each arm, a tiny happy hop with feet just off the ground, eyes closed, a huge happy smile, cheek squished against a lamb.
8. CHEER: jumping with both arms straight overhead, a lamb held high in each hand, hair bouncing, mouth wide open in a triumphant laugh.
9. LAMBY FRONT: Lamby flying toward the viewer, spinning, arms and legs flung out like a starfish, tilted about 20 degrees, bow tie fluttering, round surprised button eyes.
10. LAMBY SIDE: Lamby in side profile flying to the left, body stretched out, legs and ears trailing behind it, bow tie streaming back.
11. LAMBY FLIP: Lamby tumbling upside down, seen from the back and slightly above, legs splayed, wool puffed out, tail visible.
12. LAMBY SQUASH: Lamby hitting something, face first, squashed wide and flat like a pancake, wool puffed out at the sides, button eyes squeezed tight, tiny bow tie bent.
13. BACKUP FRONT: Backup Lamby flying toward the viewer, spinning, arms and legs flung out like a starfish, tilted about 20 degrees the other way, floppy ear flapping, mismatched button eyes.
14. BACKUP SIDE: Backup Lamby in side profile flying to the left, body stretched out, legs and the floppy ear trailing behind it.
15. BACKUP FLIP: Backup Lamby tumbling upside down, seen from the back and slightly above, legs splayed, wool puffed out.
16. BACKUP SQUASH: Backup Lamby hitting something, face first, squashed wide and flat like a pancake, wool puffed out at the sides, mismatched button eyes squeezed tight, floppy ear bent.

NEVER substitute. Do not add extra frames, merge two frames, repeat a pose, add any prop, or add the monster. If anything is unclear, draw the character in the described pose.
